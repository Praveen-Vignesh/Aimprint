Privacy & how it works

Aimprint · last updated 18 October 2026

Aimprint is a browser aim trainer built to collect research data. It is not a product with analytics bolted on — recording how you move the mouse is the point, and this page explains exactly what that means before you create an account.

What is recorded

Recording only happens while a training run is active, and only for a signed-in account. Signed out, nothing is written at all — that is enforced on the server, not just in the page.

While you are training

  • Once per rendered frame: a timestamp, the raw mouse movement counts for that frame, the camera's resulting horizontal and vertical angles, the position of the target you are engaging, and whether your crosshair was on a target.
  • Raw pointer samples between frames, at your mouse's polling rate, for finer timing.
  • For each attempt: whether it was a hit, a miss, or a timeout; how long it took you to click; how long your crosshair rested on the target first; and how far from the centre the shot landed.

About your setup

  • Your mouse DPI and in-game sensitivity, and the centimetres-per-360° they work out to.
  • Your screen resolution, pixel ratio, display refresh rate, browser user agent, and platform.
  • Which routine, difficulty and session length you chose.

Hardware details are recorded because raw mouse counts are meaningless without them — the same physical motion produces different numbers at different DPI settings.

Your account

  • Your email address, held by the authentication system so you can sign in.

What is never recorded

  • Anything outside the training page — no keystrokes, no other tabs, no clipboard, no screen or webcam capture.
  • Your mouse movement outside an active run, including on the home screen.
  • Any payment information. Aimprint is free and takes no payments.
  • Advertising or third-party tracking. There are no ad networks, no analytics scripts, and no cookies beyond the one that keeps you signed in.

Be aware: this data can identify you

The way a person moves a mouse is distinctive, in roughly the way handwriting is. Research suggests that with enough samples it can be used to tell one individual from another. That is precisely what this project studies, so you should treat the recordings as potentially identifying information about your body, not as anonymous gameplay statistics.

Please only take part if you are comfortable with that.

Why it is collected

Two research questions:

  • Telling humans from automated input. Building a classifier that can distinguish a person aiming from a script aiming — the underlying problem behind cheat detection in competitive games.
  • Whether aim can act as a behavioural signature. Investigating whether movement patterns are consistent enough within one person, and distinct enough between people, to help confirm identity.

This is independent research. Data is not sold, rented, or shared with advertisers, and no automated decision is made about you from it.

How it is stored

  • Data lives in a hosted Postgres database (Supabase), transmitted over HTTPS.
  • Your recordings attach to a pseudonymous subject identifier, kept deliberately separate from your login. The training data does not carry your email.
  • Records are append-only: neither you nor anyone else can quietly rewrite past sessions, because a reference set that can be edited is not a reference set.
  • Your own recordings are not downloadable, including by you. That is intentional — movement traces are the raw material of a behavioural signature, and an export endpoint would be a way to obtain and replay someone's. Summary statistics are what surface back to you.

Your choices

Agreement

Creating an account is how you agree to this: there is no training without one, and an account exists only because you made it. The sign-up form says so in a line next to the button, and this page is linked from there so you can read the detail first.

Withdrawing

You can stop at any time by simply not training; nothing is recorded when you are not in a run.

Deletion

Email aimprint.project@gmail.com from your registered address and ask. Two options:

  • Delete the account. This removes the link between you and your recordings, leaving the remaining data genuinely anonymous rather than merely pseudonymous.
  • Delete the recordings too. If you want the training data itself erased, say so explicitly and it will be removed.

Say which you want, because the first does not do the second.

Access

You can ask what is held about you. As above, raw movement traces are not exported, for the security reason given — a description of what exists is provided instead.

Contact

Questions, concerns, or a deletion request: aimprint.project@gmail.com.

Aimprint is a student research project, not a company. If something here is unclear or you think it is wrong, please say so — that feedback is genuinely useful.
